RMW Sort

A short training module on regulated medical waste segregation.

Five things to know

  • Only 3–5% of hospital waste should ever qualify as RMW. Anything higher means non-hazardous items are ending up in red bags.
  • The real test is saturation, not contact. An item becomes RMW when it's soaked enough to release fluid if compressed — this unit uses roughly 30% saturation as the guideline.
  • Sharps are always RMW — needles, scalpels, broken glass — no matter how clean they look.
  • Dried, flaking blood is not the same as wet saturation. Once dry and no longer releasing fluid, it usually belongs in regular waste.
  • When unsure, check the signage above the bin pair before defaulting to the red bag.
